Creator rights & responsible use

Public media does not automatically mean permission to download

A post may be visible to everyone while its creator still controls copying and reuse. Before saving media from a third-party platform, check the creator's permission, the platform's available download controls, and the rules that apply where you live.

Published September 27, 2026. Educational information, not legal advice.

Public access versus permission

A public link only indicates that the source platform allows the post to be viewed without a private invitation. It does not establish ownership, a reuse license, or permission to extract a file with an outside tool. Copyright and platform rules are separate considerations; satisfying one does not necessarily satisfy the other.

For example, a creator might allow a follower to view a public video but not allow copies to be distributed elsewhere. A post can also include music, artwork, or footage belonging to different rights holders.

Questions to ask before saving media

  1. Do you own the material? If you filmed and uploaded it, check whether it also contains third-party music or visuals.
  2. Did the creator or rights holder permit a download? Keep the permission or license terms, especially for commercial use.
  3. Does the platform provide an authorized download option? Prefer official features when available and respect settings that disable saving.
  4. Is the material access-controlled? Do not attempt to retrieve private, login-only, paywalled, or DRM-protected media with SaveDownloader.
  5. How will you use the file? Personal storage, redistribution, editing, and commercial publication can involve different permissions.

When official downloads or exports are a better choice

If you need a backup of your own account or posts, look for the platform's official download or account-data export tools first. If you are working on behalf of a creator, ask them to share the original file or an authorized export. This helps preserve quality and provides a clearer record of authorization.

SaveDownloader provides technical support for certain public links; it does not verify copyright ownership or guarantee that a particular download is permitted by the source platform.

What if a post has no download option?

Do not assume a missing download option is a technical problem to bypass. Check the original platform's rules and ask the creator for the source file or explicit permission. If an official share or embed option is available, it may meet your needs without making a separate copy.

What SaveDownloader does and does not do

SaveDownloader accepts supported public links and attempts to identify publicly available media. It does not request your platform passwords or private login cookies, is not a permanent user-media archive, and does not support private, paywalled, or DRM-protected material. Technical availability is not legal authorization.

Its results can also fail when the source platform changes a page or a media URL expires. If you have permission to save a post but encounter a technical problem, consult our download troubleshooting guide.

What creators and rights holders can do

If you believe SaveDownloader's own pages or a supported link raise a rights concern, review our copyright and rights request process. Include the affected SaveDownloader page, the original post when relevant, and the rights involved. We do not control whether a post stays online at the source platform.

Frequently asked questions

Can I repost a video simply because it was public?

No general reposting permission follows from public visibility. Obtain the relevant rights or rely on a valid applicable legal exception.

Does giving the creator credit replace permission?

Attribution is sometimes required by a license, but credit alone does not automatically authorize copying or redistribution.

Can I use downloaded clips in advertisements?

Commercial use may require permission for the video, music, identifiable people, and other protected elements. Obtain appropriate authorization before publishing.
